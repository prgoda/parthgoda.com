## What Was Actually Agreed

On November 2, [Fresenius announced](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fresenius-and-wuxi-biologics-enter-exclusive-development-agreement-to-expand-biosimilars-pipeline-302926065.html) an exclusive development agreement with WuXi Biologics, the Chinese contract development and manufacturing organization. Fresenius gets option rights to up to 37 new biosimilar candidates over roughly the next decade — enough, [Drug Discovery Trends reported](https://www.drugdiscoverytrends.com/fresenius-picks-the-biosimilars-wuxi-biologics-builds-them-in-a-deal-covering-up-to-37-candidates/), to take its pipeline from 29 molecules today to as many as 66, aimed at originator biologics with combined annual sales exceeding €370 billion. WuXi isn't paid a lump sum: Fresenius compensates it fee-for-service, one candidate at a time, only for programs it actually selects, while keeping exclusive global commercialization rights and the call on long-term manufacturing for each one. Neither company disclosed financial terms, and Fresenius said the deal is funded from existing operating cash flow — no new financing required.

The deal lands a month after Fresenius [finished buying out the remaining 45% of mAbxience](https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/fresenius-se-co-fresenius-acquires-remaining-45-of-mabxience-for-up-to-750-million-taking-full-ownership-of-its-biosimilars-platform-20261001) for up to €750 million, giving it full control of the manufacturer it had partly owned for years and completing a vertically integrated biosimilars platform spanning development through commercialization. Two days after the WuXi announcement, Fresenius raised its 2026 earnings outlook on a strong third quarter. Reading the sequence as a company using a freshly consolidated platform and a confident balance sheet to lock in a decade of low-cash-outlay pipeline growth is an interpretation the source reporting doesn't attribute to Fresenius directly — but it's the one the timing supports.

## Who It Affects

For WuXi Biologics, the deal locks in a marquee Western anchor client for the back half of the decade. That matters because, unlike its formerly affiliated company WuXi AppTec — added to the Pentagon's list of restricted Chinese firms in June 2026 — WuXi Biologics has not itself been named on that list, and a long-dated contract with a major European healthcare group is a vote of confidence other Western clients may watch closely. For Sandoz and Celltrion, the two incumbent biosimilar leaders Fresenius is chasing, the deal is a signal of future competition rather than an immediate one: neither the financial terms nor any clinical timelines for the 37 candidates have been disclosed, so how soon any of them reach patients remains unclear. Markus Manns, a portfolio manager at Union Investment, called the agreement ["strategically very significant"](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/fresenius-doubles-down-biosimilars-big-wuxi-alliance), arguing it positions Fresenius alongside Sandoz among biosimilar leaders from around 2030 — his read, not a Fresenius claim.

## What to Watch Next

Three things would turn this from an option contract into an actual pipeline: how many of the 37 candidates Fresenius exercises rather than lets lapse; whether production stays with WuXi or shifts to Fresenius's own mAbxience plants once a candidate clears early development, a choice the agreement leaves entirely to Fresenius; and whether Fresenius discloses even one figure — upfront, milestone, or royalty — once the first candidates are named, since every number attached to this deal so far is a ceiling, not a commitment.
