## What Was Actually Agreed

On October 26, [Bank of America, Citi, Morgan Stanley and Blackstone announced](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bank-of-america-blackstone-citi-and-morgan-stanley-announce-landmark-63-billion-financing-structure-to-enable-ai-compute-capacity-in-partnership-with-broadcom-302919849.html) a $63 billion financing structure built around Broadcom's AI chips for Anthropic. A newly formed special-purpose vehicle will buy Broadcom's AI accelerators and networking gear, then lease the hardware to Anthropic, with deliveries starting in 2027 under a compute-capacity agreement the two struck in April. Bank of America, Citi and Morgan Stanley arrange the SPV's senior "first-lien" debt; Blackstone alone backs the junior "second-lien" piece. Broadcom backstops the senior debt with what the release calls residual value support — a promise to cover losses up to the first-lien tranche if Anthropic can't pay. [Reuters, citing people familiar with the deal](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/wall-street-banks-blackstone-line-up-63-billion-to-finance-broadcom-chips-for-anthropic-4973662), put that tranche at roughly $43.5 billion, unconfirmed by either company.

## Why Now

As the timeline below shows, this is the second time in a month that financing for Anthropic's compute has grown bigger and more layered. The proximate trigger is Anthropic's own IPO paperwork, filed October 1, which disclosed that [Broadcom had agreed to lend Anthropic up to $42 billion](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/06/broadcom-agrees-to-expanded-chip-deals-with-google-anthropic.html) through a convertible note, funding roughly a third of a five-year, $125.2 billion commitment to lease Broadcom-built TPU capacity. Anthropic itself flagged "potential conflicts of interest" in Broadcom acting as its chip supplier, lessor, and now lender all at once. A separate, bank-led vehicle moves some of that concentrated exposure off Broadcom's balance sheet and onto Wall Street's, just as IPO investors start asking about it.

## What Each Side Gets, and Gives Up

Broadcom turns a compute commitment into revenue today without carrying all the financing risk alone — but it still backstops the SPV's senior debt, on top of the $42 billion it separately lends Anthropic. The banks collect fees and yield on $63 billion of new debt tied to one customer's ability to pay a long-dated lease. Blackstone, already a lender on an earlier $35 billion tranche of the same program (Broadcom backstopped up to $29 billion of that one), adds another leveraged bet on compounding AI demand. Anthropic gets chips without issuing more equity ahead of its IPO, but takes on a fixed, multiyear lease, reinforced by cash already pledged into a restricted account for Broadcom's benefit.

## Who It Affects, What to Watch

[OpenAI is reportedly negotiating](https://forkast.news/broadcoms-financing-web-spans-both-biggest-ai-labs-the-deal-isnt-done/) a similar Broadcom-arranged package for its own chips — unconfirmed, but if it lands, this becomes an industry template rather than a one-off. Nvidia now competes with vendor-backed financing, not just raw performance, for the same labs' chip budgets. Reading this as Wall Street quietly absorbing AI-compute credit risk that chipmakers don't want to hold alone is an interpretation, not something any party has stated — but it lines up with [Bank of America's own credit-research arm cutting Broadcom's bond rating](https://www.tradingpedia.com/2026/08/11/broadcom-debt-outlook-cut-on-ai-financing-risks/) to Marketweight in August, modeling a scenario where the guarantee platform's exposure could reach $370 billion by 2029 at 20 gigawatts — a projected ceiling, not a current number, but a sign risk is being priced even by banks underwriting this deal. Watch whether rating agencies move on Broadcom as more tranches stack up, whether Anthropic's post-IPO financials cover the leases unassisted, and whether other labs land their own bank-backed SPVs.
