## What Was Actually Agreed

On October 19, [Palantir and Knox Systems announced a partnership](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/palantir-partners-with-knox-systems-for-federal-cloud-services-93CH-4957037) to speed outside software into government environments that require FedRAMP or Department of War Impact Level 5 (IL5) authorization. The deal pairs Knox's pre-authorized federal cloud — which Knox says can clear FedRAMP/IL5 authorization in about 90 days, against a process that routinely runs a year or more — with [Apollo](https://blog.palantir.com/palantir-apollo-powering-saas-where-no-saas-has-gone-before-7be3e565c379), the continuous-delivery platform Palantir built for its own classified and air-gapped deployments and has sold externally since 2021. Neither company disclosed financial terms, duration, or exclusivity. Knox says more than 100 applications already run on its platform; trade coverage separately puts its authorizations at 16 and its customers near 70, figures the release doesn't reconcile.

In July 2025, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/10/knox-lands-6-5m-to-compete-with-palantir-in-the-federal-compliance-market/) that Knox's pitch to its first outside investors was explicitly to out-run Palantir on compliance speed — language that made the two companies rivals, not partners. Since then Knox raised a $25 million Series A whose backers included Microsoft's venture arm, M12, and the [US General Services Administration opened 2026](https://www.globalgovernmentforum.com/us-general-services-administration-sets-out-ai-ambitions-for-2026-and-war-department-adopts-new-ai-tool/) by calling the existing federal cloud-authorization process "slow and costly," rolling out a faster FedRAMP track to fix it. Reading this deal as Knox trading "beat Palantir on speed" for "sell that speed to Palantir's customers" is an interpretation neither company stated, but it's the most direct read of the public record available.

## What Each Side Gets

Knox contributes the authorization speed and an already-accredited federal cloud; Palantir contributes Apollo's delivery infrastructure and its base of accredited government customers — Lockheed Martin has used Apollo on the Aegis combat system, and Anduril has evaluated it for Longbow. Missing from both companies' statements: price, exclusivity, and whether either side can resell the other's infrastructure beyond this FedRAMP/IL5 use case.

## Who It Affects

Knox's platform runs on top of AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud's government regions, and this deal positions Knox and Palantir, not the hyperscalers directly, as the faster front door into those same clouds — pressuring AWS GovCloud, Azure Government, and Google Public Sector's own compliance-speed pitches. Software vendors selling into defense and civilian agencies benefit if the 90-day claim holds for new entrants; Knox already counts Adobe, Sierra, and Armis as clients. Palantir's government-integrator rivals, including Booz Allen, SAIC, and Leidos, face a shorter runway if outside software reaches agencies without routing through them.

## What to Watch Next

Three things would turn this from a press release into something real: a named software vendor confirming it reached FedRAMP/IL5 production through the Knox-Palantir pipeline in close to 90 days; Palantir's next government-revenue disclosure crediting faster vendor onboarding to Apollo; and whether AWS, Microsoft, or Google answer with a compliance-speed partnership of their own rather than leaning on existing GovCloud sales relationships.
