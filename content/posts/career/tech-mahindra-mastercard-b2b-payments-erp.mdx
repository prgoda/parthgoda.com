## What Was Actually Agreed

On October 26, [Tech Mahindra and Mastercard announced a partnership](https://www.mahindra.com/news-room/press-release/en/tech-mahindra-and-mastercard-partner-to-accelerate-embedded-b2b-payments-through-erp-and-procurement-platform-integration) to build Mastercard's virtual-card and embedded-payment capabilities directly into the ERP and procurement platforms enterprises already run. Tech Mahindra, the IT-services arm of India's Mahindra Group, does the system integration, workflow design, and rollout; Mastercard supplies the card network, payment rails, and fraud and reconciliation tooling. The pitch: a purchase approved inside a procurement system gets paid through a virtual card from inside that same screen, instead of being exported to a separate banking portal for someone to key in by hand.

Roshan Shetty, who leads Tech Mahindra's BFSI and public-sector business for the Americas, put the logic plainly: "Enterprises should not have to choose between modern payment capabilities and the technology investments they already rely on." Mastercard's Rebecca Meeker, SVP of B2B Platforms and Embedded Finance, described the aim as turning commercial payments from a manual task into a native part of procurement and finance workflows. Beyond that, the [announcement](https://www.mahindra.com/news-room/press-release/en/tech-mahindra-and-mastercard-partner-to-accelerate-embedded-b2b-payments-through-erp-and-procurement-platform-integration) is thin: no ERP vendor, no launch customer, no dollar figure, and no timeline were disclosed. [Coverage in Fintech Magazine](https://fintechmagazine.com/news/how-tech-mahindra-mastercard-speed-up-embedded-b2b-payment) and [IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/tech-mahindra-and-mastercard-partner-to-bring-b2b-payments-into-erp/) both note the release mentions "agentic AI" only in passing, without saying what an AI agent would actually be allowed to do — or whether one could ever initiate a payment on its own.

In April 2025, Mastercard's path into an ERP ran through a bank: it [launched a program](https://fintechmagazine.com/news/how-tech-mahindra-mastercard-speed-up-embedded-b2b-payment) letting banks switch on virtual-card payments inside partner platforms like ERPs and expense tools without a direct integration. This deal skips that step entirely, putting Mastercard and an IT consultancy in the room together instead. Reading that as Mastercard hedging against banks controlling the ERP relationship is an interpretation, not something either company said, but it lines up with a second fact: Mastercard spent 2026 building [Agent Pay for machines](https://etherworld.co/mastercard-launches-agent-pay-for-machines-to-power-ai-driven-commerce/), infrastructure for AI agents to transact without a human at checkout. Getting a card physically embedded in the procurement software before agentic buying arrives is the more defensible position than retrofitting it afterward.

## Who It Affects

Tech Mahindra's rivals — Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant — have deep ERP-implementation practices of their own and every incentive to cut similar deals with Visa or Amex rather than cede embedded B2B payments to one network and one integrator. Banks that adopted Mastercard's 2025 enablement program now compete with their own card network for the same ERP real estate. And procurement-software vendors gain a payment layer they didn't have to build, at the cost of a dependency on terms Mastercard and Tech Mahindra set without them in the room.

## What to Watch Next

Whether Mastercard or Tech Mahindra ever names a specific ERP platform or a live customer; whether either discloses commercial terms; and whether a later announcement says anything concrete about what an AI agent is permitted to do inside this flow, rather than gesturing at "agentic AI" as a feature still to come.
