## What Was Actually Agreed

On October 15, [AstraZeneca and the AI drug-discovery company insitro announced a strategic collaboration](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/astrazeneca-announces-new-strategic-collaboration-with-insitro-for-difficult-to-treat-cardiometabolic-diseases.html) to find new drug targets for MASH (a liver disease formerly called NASH) and cardiomyopathies — a group of heart-muscle diseases. Both have resisted treatment partly because their root biological drivers are hard to pin down. insitro will run its "Virtual Human" platform — large-scale human and cellular data paired with machine learning — to map disease biology and advance AstraZeneca-nominated targets through development-candidate selection. AstraZeneca then takes over clinical development, regulatory work, and commercialization, and holds exclusive worldwide rights to whatever programs it selects out of the collaboration.

## What Each Side Gets, and Gives Up

insitro gets $40 million now, up to roughly $1.46 billion more tied to development and commercial milestones, and single-digit royalties if a drug reaches market — but gives up exclusivity on anything AstraZeneca selects across both diseases. AstraZeneca gets first claim on insitro's output in MASH and cardiomyopathies without building a target-discovery platform of its own, but commits years of potential milestone payments to targets that don't exist yet, on a platform that — like every AI-drug-discovery company's — has not yet produced an approved medicine for anyone.

## Why Now

Two pressures line up. insitro [cut 22% of its staff in May 2025](https://www.statnews.com/2025/05/08/insitro-ai-biotech-daphne-koller-staff-layoffs/) to extend its cash runway into 2027, which sharpens the appeal of a well-funded pharma validation deal even on mostly contingent terms. It's insitro's third such pact, after collaborations with Gilead (2019, on NASH) and Bristol Myers Squibb (2020, on ALS and frontotemporal dementia). MASH, the deal's lead indication, has also gotten more contested: Madrigal's Rezdiffra remains the only approved MASH drug, but Novo Nordisk's Wegovy picked up a US MASH indication in 2025 and added a UK approval in July 2026, broadening the competitive field before AstraZeneca has a comparable asset of its own. Betting on insitro's platform for a novel target, rather than following the GLP-1 drugs into the same disease, is one way to enter without arriving last.

## Who It Affects

Madrigal and Novo Nordisk compete for the market insitro and AstraZeneca's work ultimately targets, though on a timeline of years, not quarters. insitro's AI-drug-discovery peers — Recursion (which absorbed Exscientia) and Alphabet's Isomorphic Labs — face an implicit comparison: none of the three platforms has yet carried an AI-originated candidate to approval, and this deal is AstraZeneca's bet that insitro's gets there first.

## What to Watch Next

The deal's real test isn't the announcement, it's whether insitro ever collects on the $1.46 billion. Watch for a named development candidate (the trigger for the first real milestone payment), whether AstraZeneca exercises its selection rights on more than one program, and whether insitro signs a comparable deal with another drugmaker in the next year — a sign the model is generating results rather than just extending the runway.
