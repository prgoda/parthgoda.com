## What Was Actually Agreed

On October 13, [Adani Enterprises and Jabil signed a non-binding memorandum of understanding](https://www.adani.com/newsroom/media-releases/adani-enterprises-jabil-sign-mou-for-50-50-data-centre-hardware-joint-venture) for a proposed 50:50 joint venture to manufacture and assemble AI data center hardware in India: racks, servers, storage, networking gear, power distribution units, and liquid-cooling systems. Both companies' releases describe the target as a multi-gigawatt manufacturing platform aimed at a global AI-infrastructure market they size at more than $3 trillion over the next seven years. Neither side disclosed a capital commitment, a factory location, or a timeline for the definitive agreements the MoU says must still follow, along with regulatory approvals.

This is a sequel, not an opening move. On [June 15, Adani and Jabil announced only an intent](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/adani-enterprises-jabil-target-a-strategic-alliance-to-build-ai-data-centre-infrastructure-platform-in-india/) to explore a strategic alliance, with no ownership structure or product list attached. Four months later, the MoU supplies both: a 50:50 split and a defined hardware scope. It's still short of a binding deal, but it's a concrete step up from a joint press release with no numbers in it.

The commercial logic is straightforward: Adani has committed [$100 billion to build 5 gigawatts](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/17/india-adani-ai-data-centers-investment.html) of AI-ready data centers in India by 2035, and today it buys the racks, servers, and cooling systems that fill them rather than building them domestically. Jabil already runs the kind of contract-manufacturing operation — six decades of it, mostly serving electronics brands like Apple — that could build them instead.

The timing of the deeper commitment lines up with a second, less commercial story. As the timeline above shows, the June alliance was floated while Gautam Adani still faced an active US securities and wire fraud indictment, before a judge dismissed the core charges against him with prejudice on August 10, following a Justice Department motion to drop the case. Reading the October MoU as Jabil — a Nasdaq-listed company — waiting for that legal cloud to clear before committing to a 50:50 structure with Adani Enterprises is an interpretation, not something either company stated, but the sequence fits.

## Who It Affects

A domestic manufacturing platform at this scale would compete with the Taiwanese and Chinese contract manufacturers — Foxconn, Wistron, Quanta — that currently assemble most AI server hardware for hyperscalers operating in India, and with Dell, HPE, and Supermicro as branded suppliers. It also puts Adani ahead of rival Indian conglomerate Reliance, which has announced its own AI buildout but not a hardware-manufacturing arm to match. For Jabil's shareholders, the deal ties the company's AI growth story to Adani Group's broader reputation at a moment when that reputation is only recently less encumbered, not uncomplicated.

## What to Watch Next

Three things would turn this from an MoU into infrastructure: whether a binding joint-venture agreement, with a named factory site and capital figure, follows in the coming months; whether either company names an anchor hyperscaler customer for the venture's output, rather than describing the market in aggregate; and whether India's production-linked incentive scheme for electronics manufacturing extends funding to the venture once it's formally structured.
