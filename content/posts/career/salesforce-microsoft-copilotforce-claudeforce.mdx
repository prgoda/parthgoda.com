## What Was Actually Agreed

On November 17, at Microsoft Ignite, [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/copilotforce-announcement/) and [Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2026/11/17/microsoft-and-salesforce-announce-copilotforce-bringing-trusted-customer-context-and-action-into-microsoft-ai-experiences/) announced Copilotforce: a plugin that lets Microsoft Copilot read Salesforce CRM records and run sales workflows — more than 38 prebuilt skills — without leaving Copilot or Teams. A companion piece lets Salesforce Data 360 and Microsoft Fabric's OneLake share data in both directions without copying it; the Fabric-to-Salesforce direction is live now, the reverse is still in preview. The Copilot plugin is in public preview through Microsoft's and Salesforce's app directories; a Sales Cloud app inside Teams is piloting with enterprise customers ahead of an open beta "later this year," [according to coverage of the announcement](https://www.comparethecloud.net/news/salesforce-and-microsoft-bring-sales-workflows-into-copilot-and-teams-under-copilotforce). Any write-back to a Salesforce record needs explicit user confirmation, and access runs through each person's own login and existing permissions rather than a shared service account. [Accenture](https://itdaily.com/blogs/cloud/microsoft-salesforce-copilotforce-teams/) is named as design and pilot partner. Neither company disclosed pricing, a general-availability date, or adoption numbers; using it requires a qualifying Salesforce Sales Cloud edition plus an individual Microsoft 365 Copilot license per seat.

Salesforce has now built nearly this same bridge twice in under three months. As the comparison above shows, Copilotforce mirrors Claudeforce's shape closely — a prebuilt-skills plugin, a named design partner, a pilot-to-beta path — one platform and one quarter apart. For Microsoft, the timing follows a rough week: Nadella opened Ignite's keynote [acknowledging](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/satya-nadella-kicks-off-microsoft-ignite-with-an-outlook-outage-mea-culpa-we-have-work-to-do/) a multi-day Outlook and Microsoft 365 outage that had cut off email in more than 70 countries, saying "we have work to do," before pitching Copilot as the new operating system for work. Reading Copilotforce as a deliberately large announcement timed to follow that apology is an interpretation, not something either company stated, but the one-day sequencing supports it.

## What Each Side Gets, and Gives Up

Microsoft gets Salesforce's CRM data and sales motion running inside Copilot and Teams — reinforcing the pitch that Copilot, not any single model, is where enterprise work happens. Salesforce gets distribution inside the productivity suite most of its own customers already pay for, without staking its AI strategy on one lab. Neither side gives up its existing bet: Microsoft's Copilot still runs primarily on OpenAI's models, and Salesforce's own Agentforce still runs on Claude by default. What each gives up is exclusivity — Salesforce's CRM layer is now wired into two AI ecosystems that compete directly with each other, and Microsoft is building trusted-data pipelines with a company whose own flagship AI partner is Anthropic, a Microsoft and OpenAI rival.

## Who It Affects, and What to Watch

The clearest loser of attention, if not revenue, is the idea that an enterprise has to pick one AI assistant. Rivals pitching a single, closed stack — tightly bundling a CRM or productivity suite to one model provider — now have to explain why a customer shouldn't just use whichever front end is already open. Watch for three things: whether Microsoft or Salesforce discloses pricing and a GA date once the Teams pilot ends; whether Anthropic responds with its own expanded Salesforce integration rather than ceding ground; and whether this is the start of Salesforce shipping the same skills plugin to every major AI platform, turning Agentforce from a product into pure plumbing.
