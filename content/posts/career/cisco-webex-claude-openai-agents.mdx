## What Was Actually Agreed

At WebexOne 2026 in Austin on October 7, [Cisco announced](https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2026/m10/cisco-unveils-new-agentic-collaboration-experiences.html) two separate AI deals for Webex in the same breath. Anthropic's Claude is coming in as an embedded agent, built on Claude Managed Agents, able to analyze data, generate content, and carry out multi-step work using a team's shared context without leaving Webex. Separately, a new Model Context Protocol connector feeds Webex's enterprise context out to OpenAI's Dots agents — for now a one-way flow, with Cisco saying Dots will later run inside Webex as an "always-on personal agent." Neither company's own executives put out a statement alongside Cisco's, and neither integration has a disclosed price or ship date; [most of the lineup](https://uctoday.com/webexone-2026-amit-barave-on-claude-agents-the-new-webex) is slated for "early 2027."

Openness isn't new for Webex, as the timeline above shows — Cisco has let outside AI into the product since late 2025. What changed on October 7 is depth: Claude and Dots aren't read-only data integrations like the earlier ones, they're being positioned as agents that act inside the product. The pressure behind that shift is competitive. Microsoft bundles Copilot natively into Teams; Zoom and Google are building their own assistants into their own meeting products. Cisco doesn't have a frontier model to bundle, so its differentiation has to be "every model, one platform" instead.

## Who It Affects

Microsoft is the direct target, and the irony is that Copilot already sits inside Webex as a guest integration, same as Amazon Q and Salesforce — Cisco is now treating Microsoft's own AI as just one plug-in among several, not a peer. Zoom and Google Workspace face a rival collaboration platform offering the two highest-profile frontier labs natively, which raises the bar on "bring your own model" flexibility. Enterprise IT and security teams inherit the harder job: deciding what a Claude agent, a Dots connector, and a Copilot guest are each allowed to see and do inside the same meetings.

## What to Watch Next

Three things would confirm this is more than a keynote slide: whether Dots gains write access inside Webex — matching Claude's role — once its "personal agent" version ships; whether Cisco or either AI lab discloses pricing or a firm release date, neither of which has surfaced yet; and whether Microsoft responds by deepening Copilot's own hooks into rival platforms rather than keeping it Teams-exclusive.
