## What Was Actually Agreed

On November 26, [Atos and Scaleway announced](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/26/3410685/0/fr/atos-et-scaleway-s-unissent-pour-acc%C3%A9l%C3%A9rer-le-d%C3%A9ploiement-de-clouds-souverains-gr%C3%A2ce-%C3%A0-l-ia.html) that Atos has named Scaleway, the cloud arm of Xavier Niel's Iliad telecom group, as its preferred partner for deploying Genesis Intelligence — the AI software suite, including the Atos Codex coding assistant, that [Atos launched on October 26](https://www.atos.ai/en/news-insights/press-releases/atos-launches-atos-genesis-intelligence-suite-featuring-atos-codex) — inside sovereign cloud environments for large French public and private organizations. Atos is also folding Scaleway into its own Cloud Platform and Digital Engineer tooling, so customers running mixed Scaleway, on-premises, and other cloud environments can govern them from one console. Atos frames the pairing as a "100% European value chain" meant to speed migrations and cut operating costs; neither company disclosed pricing, volume commitments, or whether "preferred partner" carries any exclusivity. Atos's France operations and technology director, [Sylvain Barthelemy, said](https://www.clubic.com/actualite-641137-atos-mise-sur-le-francais-scaleway-pour-faire-migrer-les-donnees-sensibles-vers-le-cloud-souverain.html) resilience has become a strategic issue for clients that need high availability and security, while Scaleway's sales director, Doreen Pernel, described the deal as ending the trade-off between sovereignty and capability and called it a credible European alternative to the hyperscalers.

Atos spent most of 2025 and early 2026 fighting to avoid running out of cash; the July refinancing retired €857 million of its most senior debt, and net debt still stood near €2 billion at mid-year. Building its own sovereign data-center capacity from scratch was never realistic on that balance sheet — Atos needed an infrastructure partner, and specifically one it could market as purely European rather than one running on licensed Microsoft or Google technology underneath. Scaleway, for its part, is leading a roughly $10 billion bid for one of the EU's planned AI "gigafactories" and needs large, regulated enterprise customers to prove its infrastructure can carry more than startups and public-sector pilots. As the timeline above shows, the deal lands four months after Atos's sovereign-cloud pivot and exactly one month after Genesis Intelligence shipped — pairing the software with infrastructure was the obvious next move once both pieces existed independently.

## What Each Side Gets, and Gives Up

Atos gets a sovereign-infrastructure story to attach to its own AI software without taking on the capital cost of building data centers itself; it gives up control over the compute layer underneath its flagship AI product. Scaleway gets a distribution channel into Atos's existing enterprise and public-sector accounts that it would otherwise have to build itself; it gives up some of its own brand distinctiveness, since its infrastructure will now be sold partly under Atos's name. Neither company disclosed financial terms or named a single live customer.

## Who It Affects, and What to Watch

The deal sharpens a split in France's sovereign-cloud market, shown in the diagram above: Atos and Scaleway are positioning themselves as the "built in Europe" option against Bleu (Orange and Capgemini, running licensed Microsoft Azure technology) and S3NS (Thales and Google Cloud's joint venture). That distinction comes with a real gap — [S3NS already holds the ANSSI SecNumCloud security qualification](https://www.qovery.com/blog/bleu-s3ns-scaleway-french-trusted-cloud-saas-scaleup-2026) that Scaleway is still pursuing. Reading this deal as Atos choosing speed-to-market over waiting for that certification is an interpretation, not something either company stated. Watch whether Scaleway closes the SecNumCloud gap, whether Atos names actual production customers rather than describing the partnership in the abstract, and whether Bleu or S3NS respond with their own software-plus-infrastructure bundling to match it.
