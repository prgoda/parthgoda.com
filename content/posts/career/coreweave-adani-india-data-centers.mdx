## What Was Actually Agreed

On October 6, [CoreWeave announced its first data centers in India](https://www.coreweave.com/news/coreweave-enters-india-expanding-ai-cloud-platform-with-adaniconnex): a 240-megawatt campus across three buildings at AdaniConneX's Taloja site in Navi Mumbai. CoreWeave will be the sole tenant, running Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform inside buildings that AdaniConneX designs, finances, and builds — not CoreWeave. The two hold an option to double the campus to 480 megawatts. The first phase is due online in mid-2028. Neither side disclosed what CoreWeave is paying or for how long.

AdaniConneX — the data-center joint venture between Adani Enterprises and EdgeConneX — isn't new to this role. [Google committed to a roughly $15 billion, five-year AI campus](https://container-news.com/adani-and-google-partner-to-build-data-centre-in-visakhapatnam) built by AdaniConneX and Airtel in Visakhapatnam, announced in October 2025, with Adani's own stake [reported at up to $5 billion](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/indias-adani-group-set-to-invest-up-to-5bn-in-google-data-center-campus-in-visakhapatnam/). CoreWeave's deal follows the identical shape: the cloud brings chips and customers; AdaniConneX brings land, power, and construction.

The timeline runs back to February 2026, when Adani and Reliance each laid out decade-long AI infrastructure plans within days of each other: Adani's [$100 billion push](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/17/india-adani-ai-data-centers-investment.html) toward 5 gigawatts of capacity, and Reliance's [$110 billion plan](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/19/reliance-unveils-110b-ai-investment-plan-as-india-ramps-up-tech-ambitions/). Both conglomerates already held land, power contracts, and capital that a cloud provider entering a new country would otherwise spend years assembling, and both have since recruited foreign tenants to prove the model works: Reliance landed [Meta's first India data-center commitment](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/10/meta-signs-first-ai-data-center-deal-in-india-with-reliance/) in June.

For CoreWeave, the deal extends a pattern already visible in Europe — a [co-location deal with Conapto in Sweden](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/product-news/coreweave-partners-with-conapto-to-expand-ai-cloud-capacity-in-sweden-powered-by-renewable-energy/), where it also leased finished space rather than building it. As the timeline above shows, CoreWeave is scaling internationally through local landlords, not its own construction crews. Reading this as CoreWeave judging it needed an India footprint now rather than later is an interpretation, not something either company stated, but it matches CoreWeave's broader race to add power faster than it can build alone.

## What Each Side Gets

CoreWeave gets an India footprint without the multi-year process of securing land and power itself, plus a local office in a market where the government's IndiaAI Mission is actively courting foreign AI infrastructure. AdaniConneX gets a second marquee tenant to point to as it chases its $100 billion target; COO Sachin Jain's statement frames the deal as bringing "240 megawatts of capacity, NVIDIA Vera Rubin, and CoreWeave's expertise" to AI builders in India. Missing from both statements: the price. No lease value, term, or exclusivity was disclosed.

## Who It Affects

Reliance Jio, running the same landlord-tenant playbook with Meta, now has a rival at the same scale rather than a head start. Microsoft, AWS, and Google's own directly built India regions face a faster, lower-commitment alternative other AI clouds could copy instead of matching their balance-sheet approach. And smaller neoclouds — Nebius, Lambda, Crusoe — have a visible template for entering India through a conglomerate's data-center arm rather than building alone.

## What to Watch Next

Whether CoreWeave exercises the option to double the campus to 480 megawatts once the first phase is running; whether the mid-2028 timeline survives India's power-grid and import constraints on Nvidia's newest systems; and whether AdaniConneX signs a third foreign cloud tenant, which would confirm it as India's default AI-infrastructure landlord rather than a partner Google and CoreWeave happened to share.
