## What Was Actually Agreed

On November 9, [Adobe and Disney announced](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/11/adobe-and-the-walt-disney-company-alliance) a multi-year strategic alliance naming Adobe as Disney's "Official AI Innovation Partner." The mechanism is Adobe's Firefly Foundry, a product for building custom generative models trained on a client's own material; here, Disney's characters, environments, and visual styles. The resulting models stay proprietary to Disney rather than feeding a general-purpose public model, and Disney gets early access to Firefly, Premiere, and Photoshop plus dedicated Adobe engineering support. Adobe's Hannah Elsakr, VP of GenAI New Business Ventures, called it the chapter where the companies "build a new creative foundation that thoughtfully deploys AI as an accelerant for artistic vision, creativity and guest engagement." Neither side disclosed what the deal is worth.

The Adobe relationship itself is an expansion, not a debut: it grows out of a narrower deal struck in June, extending Firefly Foundry from theme-park concept art into The Walt Disney Studios' StudioLAB, where initial work targets post-production and visual effects, and into ESPN Edge, where the companies are exploring ways to repurpose ESPN's archive. In exchange, Adobe gets marketing rights across Walt Disney World, Disneyland, Disney Cruise Line, ESPN Edge, and StudioLAB — five consumer-facing Disney units Adobe can now use in its own storytelling.

The timeline above is the real story. Since mid-2025, Disney has been sorting every AI company that touches its characters into one of two piles: a deal, or a legal letter. It sued [Midjourney alongside NBCUniversal](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/animation-movies/disney-and-universal-call-ai-bottomless-pit-of-plagiarism-in-lawsuit-against-midjourney) for training on its catalog without permission; it sent Google a [cease-and-desist over Veo and Imagen outputs](https://www.thewrap.com/disney-google-cease-and-desist-ai-copyright-infringement/) depicting Mickey Mouse and Star Wars characters, and reportedly sent similar letters to Meta and Character.AI. The same week it warned Google, it signed the opposite kind of deal with OpenAI: a [$1 billion investment and a license for 200 characters](https://decrypt.co/352005/disney-targets-google-over-ai-copyright-violations-1-billion-openai-deal) to appear in Sora and ChatGPT. Reading the Adobe alliance as the third leg of that same sorting exercise — the partner trusted to build tools that keep Disney's own IP in Disney's own hands, as opposed to the partner licensed to put that IP in front of the public — is an interpretation, not something Disney has stated outright, but it fits the pattern the company has been building in public for a year.

## What Each Side Gets, and Who It Affects

Disney gets production tooling trained on its own assets that, per the companies, never leaves its control — speed in park design, post-production, and sports content without handing a third party's general-purpose model a stake in its characters. Adobe gets a marquee enterprise reference for Firefly Foundry, its custom-model product, at a moment when investors have questioned whether Adobe's AI story can compete with OpenAI, Google, and Midjourney on the consumer side; it also gets to market itself across Disney's parks, cruise line, and ESPN. The clearest losers are the AI companies still on the cease-and-desist side of Disney's line: Google, Midjourney, Meta, and Character.AI gain nothing from this deal and remain exposed to the posture it reinforces. Other studios now have a public template — in-house tool vendor, licensed distribution partner, lawsuits for everyone else — to copy or react to.

## What to Watch Next

Three things would confirm this is working rather than just branding: whether Adobe or Disney ever discloses usage or revenue tied to the relationship, given Adobe's need to show its AI bets paying off; whether StudioLAB's "initial" post-production projects actually ship something audiences can see, rather than staying confined to parks; and whether the Midjourney lawsuit reaches a resolution that either validates or undercuts the legal theory Disney is using against Google, Meta, and Character.AI in parallel.
