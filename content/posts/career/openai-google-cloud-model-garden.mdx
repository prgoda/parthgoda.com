## What Was Actually Agreed

On October 21, [OpenAI and Google Cloud announced](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/21/openai-to-sell-its-top-models-on-google-cloud-in-latest-enterprise-push) that OpenAI's frontier models — reported as GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, and GPT-6 Luna, though naming varied by outlet — plus its Codex coding agent are coming to Google Cloud's Model Garden, the console enterprises already use to buy Gemini and other third-party models. The rollout starts as an invite-only private preview; general availability is promised "in the coming weeks," with no firm date, pricing, or region list disclosed. Enterprises will buy access through Google Cloud Marketplace, applying existing Google Cloud spending commitments to one consolidated invoice. Google says customer prompts and outputs stay inside each customer's own data perimeter and are never used to train Google's models; stronger controls — zero data retention, confidential compute — are still "planned," not live. The models are also due to reach Google's Antigravity coding tool and Gemini Agent platform "soon."

This isn't the first OpenAI-Google Cloud arrangement — Google has sold OpenAI raw compute capacity since 2025. What's new is the direction of the transaction: Google Cloud becomes a storefront selling OpenAI's product to enterprise customers, shelved next to Google's own competing Gemini models.

## Why Now

As the timeline below shows, October 21 is the last domino in a chain that started well before this month. Microsoft's exclusive right to OpenAI's technology ended on April 27, 2026; Sam Altman said at the time that Microsoft remains OpenAI's "primary cloud partner," but OpenAI could now sell across any cloud. OpenAI used that opening to add AWS the same month. Google Cloud — which had separately pushed regulators to unwind Microsoft's exclusivity — was the more complicated next addition, since Google also sells Gemini, OpenAI's most direct competitor. That it happened anyway suggests OpenAI's hunger for distribution now outweighs its reluctance to hand a rival a cut of its sales, or, read the other way, that Google would rather earn a margin on a competitor's product than cede the sale entirely. Neither company has said which motive weighed more; the two aren't mutually exclusive.

## Who It Affects

Microsoft is the most exposed party. Azure remains OpenAI's default home, but it's no longer the only one, and every dollar spent on GPT models through Google Cloud is a dollar Azure doesn't collect directly — even though Microsoft still receives its revenue share from OpenAI through 2030 on undisclosed terms. Anthropic, whose Claude already sits in Model Garden, now shares shelf space with both of its biggest rivals, with Google acting as an open marketplace rather than picking a side. Enterprise customers already holding Google Cloud spending commitments gain a reason to route AI spending there instead of opening a separate Azure or AWS relationship — the switching-cost lock-in cloud providers prize.

## What to Watch Next

The test is whether "coming weeks" turns into an actual general-availability date with published pricing and region support, since neither exists yet. Watch also for whether OpenAI's revenue-share obligations to Microsoft extend to sales made through Google Cloud, and whether Google ever discloses what cut it takes for reselling a rival's models — a number that would say more about what this deal is actually worth than either company's announcement has so far.
