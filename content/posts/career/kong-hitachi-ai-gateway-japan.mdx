## What Was Actually Agreed

On November 6, Hitachi and the API infrastructure company Kong each confirmed a strategic partnership agreement in their own releases, filed separately through Japan's PR Times wire: [Kong's announcement](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000084.000129151.html) describes a signed partner contract with Hitachi, Ltd.; [Hitachi's announcement](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000648.000067590.html) frames it as expanding an "AI agent integration solution" built on the collaboration. [IT Business Today](https://itbusinesstoday.com/tech/ai/kong-and-hitachi-sign-strategic-partnership-agreement/) independently reported the same terms: Hitachi contributes its open-source systems-integration experience and its ability to build and manage enterprise systems; Kong contributes its API and AI connectivity products — Kong Gateway, Konnect, Kong AI Gateway, and Insomnia — covering design, testing, deployment, and monitoring, on-premises or in the cloud. The named targets are two sectors where Hitachi already runs large accounts: manufacturing, pitched around physical-AI systems that use machinery and sensor data for maintenance and quality control, and financial services, pitched around securing and governing APIs as banks connect new AI applications to core systems. Neither company disclosed financial terms or exclusivity, and coverage of both releases points only to "joint customer proposals and technical validation" as next steps — no production customer has been named yet.

## Why Now

The timing lines up with a live example of the exact problem the partnership claims to solve. Two days earlier, Kong [disclosed](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000083.000129151.html) that Mizuho Financial Group had adopted Kong AI Gateway as the connection-management layer behind a push to run AI agents by the thousands — a customer deployment, not a partnership, but independently [reported by Mynavi](https://news.mynavi.jp/techplus/article/20261104-5254134) and the clearest evidence yet that a major Japanese institution now needs exactly what Kong sells: a way to authenticate, monitor, and rate-limit agents at a scale no one managing APIs by hand could keep up with.

Kong arrives already carrying the credibility of a [$2 billion valuation](https://balderton.com/?p=10910) from its November 2024 funding round, raised specifically on the thesis that it would become "AI connectivity" infrastructure rather than just an API gateway vendor. Hitachi, meanwhile, has spent the past two years stacking AI partners — Google Cloud, OpenAI, Nvidia — into its Lumada platform without, on the public record, owning a comparable connectivity and governance layer of its own. Pairing with a specialist is a faster, cheaper way to close that gap than building one in-house.

## What Each Side Gets

For Kong, the deal buys distribution it cannot build alone: access to Hitachi's existing manufacturing and banking relationships across Japan, a market where enterprise software usually sells through a trusted integrator rather than direct from a foreign vendor. For Hitachi, it buys a ready-made, API-level governance layer to slot under its own AI-agent push, without the multi-year cost of building equivalent gateway and monitoring software internally. What Hitachi gives up is margin and the direct customer relationship on the connectivity layer itself; what Kong gives up is control over how its products get priced and positioned once Hitachi is reselling them inside larger systems-integration contracts.

## Who It Affects

The direct competitors are the other API-management vendors chasing the same enterprise-AI wave in Japan — Google's Apigee, Salesforce's MuleSoft, and the cloud providers' own gateway products — all now facing a rival bundled with one of Japan's largest systems integrators instead of sold standalone. Hitachi's existing AI partners, Google Cloud and OpenAI among them, aren't directly threatened, since neither competes with Kong at the connectivity layer, but the deal shows Hitachi assembling its AI stack from several single-purpose vendors rather than one full-stack provider. Japanese manufacturers and banks already working with Hitachi are the intended beneficiaries: a packaged option instead of having to evaluate and integrate API-governance tooling themselves.

## What to Watch Next

Three signals would separate this from a press release: a named manufacturing or financial customer running the joint solution in production, not just a "planned" proposal; whether Hitachi extends the same Kong-based stack to accounts outside Japan, turning a regional reseller deal into a global one; and whether Mizuho's agent rollout — the backdrop this partnership is selling into — holds up at scale without the governance failures API gateways exist to prevent.
