## What Was Actually Agreed

On October 27, [AMD said it would invest $1 billion](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1302/amd-and-meta-advance-strategic-partnership-to-power-metas-superintelligence-infrastructure) in the stock offering Meta had launched the day before to help fund its AI spending, and gave an update on the rest of the partnership: initial AMD Helios rack-scale systems are now installed and being tested at Meta ahead of a broader rollout, Meta has become the lead customer for AMD's sixth-generation EPYC "Venice" server chips, and AMD EPYC Turin processors already run the CPU-only virtual machines behind Meta's Muse personal AI agent.

AMD wasn't the only chipmaker writing a check. [Nvidia committed $3 billion](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-to-invest-3-bln-in-meta-share-offering-as-part-of-bigger-ai-partnership-4974236) to the same raise, tied to its own expanded Meta deal covering Vera Rubin GPUs and Vera CPUs, and Broadcom joined as a third buyer. Meta ultimately [priced $25 billion of common stock and $12.5 billion of preferred stock](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/42149) — $37.5 billion, after orders ran roughly five times the shares on offer.

Terms AMD didn't disclose: when it will actually buy the Meta shares, at what price, or a timeline for moving Helios from "testing" to the broad deployment the companies described. Meta, for its part, said a Muse spokesperson, designed the agent's system "to be largely independent of specific CPUs" — a hedge against reading too much exclusivity into any one supplier's chips.

As the timeline shows, the direction of the money has flipped since February. Back then, Meta was the one getting equity — a performance-based warrant for up to 160 million AMD shares, roughly 10% of the company, vesting only as AMD shipped hardware and hit stock-price hurdles. Eight months later, with Meta's own AI capital spending climbing and investors growing twitchier about the bill, Meta is the one raising cash, and the chipmakers it depends on are among the buyers. Reading the October round as AMD and Nvidia propping up demand for the stock sale that funds their own biggest orders is an interpretation analysts have already made openly: outlets covering the deal called the structure "circular financing," the same label critics pinned on Meta's February stake in AMD. Meta's shares, [per 24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/28/metas-25-billion-stock-sale-drew-five-times-the-demand/), closed below the $625 offering price on the day it priced.

## Who It Affects

Oracle, Microsoft, and OpenAI — the other three customers AMD has said are running early Helios systems — now sit alongside Meta as proof points AMD needs to convert from pilots into recurring revenue. Nvidia, still holding roughly 95% of the data-center GPU market by one estimate, is answering AMD's bet on Meta's AI agent with its own: its $3 billion buy-in comes bundled with a Vera Rubin rollout and closer work on Muse. Meta's other suppliers and its shareholders face the same open question either way — whether an AI buildout substantially financed by the companies that profit from it produces revenue as durable as an arm's-length customer's would.

## What to Watch Next

Three signals will tell whether this is infrastructure or just mutual stock support: whether AMD and Nvidia actually settle their Meta purchases rather than letting the commitments lapse; whether Helios moves from "testing" to the volume deployment AMD has promised, on a disclosed timeline; and whether Meta's stock recovers above its $625 offering price, which would suggest investors read the supplier buy-ins as validation rather than as chipmakers propping up their own order book.
