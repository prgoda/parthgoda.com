## What Was Actually Agreed

On November 3, [Klarna announced](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/72468364/klarna-expands-partnership-with-mollie-bringing-enhanced-dispute-management-and-consumer-fx-to-merchants-across-europe?publisherId=58763726) an expanded contract with Mollie, the Dutch payment service provider it first partnered with [back in 2018](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/mollie-and-klarna-partner-where-simplicity-meets-flexibility/) to make Klarna's buy-now-pay-later options available to Mollie's merchants. The new agreement extends two of Klarna's newer products — Consumer FX, a no-exchange-fee currency-conversion tool it [switched on across its network on October 21](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/klarna-launches-consumer-fx-for-shoppers-buying-across-borders), and an enhanced dispute-management flow — to all of Mollie's merchants, through every way they already connect to Mollie, including direct API, hosted payment page, and its platform-partner plug-in ecosystem. [PaymentsJournal](https://www.paymentsjournal.com/klarna-broadens-european-merchant-footprint-with-mollie-expansion/amp/) independently reported the same terms. Neither company disclosed financial terms, and neither release claims exclusivity.

## Why Now

The timing follows Mollie's own expansion. On September 1, Mollie [closed its acquisition of GoCardless](https://www.fintechfutures.com/m-a/mollie-acquires-gocardless), reported at roughly $1.1 billion (some outlets cite the figure in euros), pushing its combined merchant base past 350,000 — well beyond the 55,000 SME and enterprise merchants Mollie had across the Netherlands, Germany, Austria, and Finland when the original Klarna deal was signed in 2018. Extending the Klarna relationship across that larger base, rather than rebuilding it merchant by merchant, is the faster way for Klarna to turn Mollie's growth into its own distribution. It also lands less than two weeks after Klarna activated Consumer FX. Klarna's release cites DHL data putting the share of shoppers who buy across borders at 70% in 2026, which it describes as a 10% increase over 2025 — the kind of trend a new feature can ride faster through an existing partner's merchant base than by selling it to each merchant directly.

## What Each Side Gets

For Klarna, the deal is close to pure distribution: a flagship, weeks-old product reaches hundreds of thousands of merchants without a dedicated merchant-by-merchant sales push, at the cost of leaving the direct merchant relationship — and whatever share of the transaction economics that implies — with Mollie rather than Klarna. For Mollie, it is a way to compete on payment-method breadth against larger processors like Adyen and Stripe without building FX or dispute-management infrastructure of its own, in exchange for its merchants staying dependent on Klarna's technology to run both.

## Who It Affects

Other buy-now-pay-later providers — Affirm, Clearpay, PayPal's Pay Later — now compete for the same payment-service-provider shelf space Klarna just locked up at Mollie, including whatever share of Mollie's enlarged merchant base came in through GoCardless. Processors like Adyen, Stripe, and Checkout.com face a Mollie that can now match a Klarna-plus-FX-plus-disputes bundle without building the equivalent in-house. Mollie's merchants, in turn, get both tools automatically through their existing integration, which is also a reason for them to stay on Mollie rather than shop for a processor that would require rebuilding the connection from scratch.

## What to Watch Next

Three things would separate this from a reach number in a press release: whether Klarna discloses actual merchant adoption, not just eligibility; whether Mollie extends comparable FX or dispute terms to rival BNPL providers, which would mean this partnership isn't functionally exclusive even though neither side calls it that; and whether Klarna's own claimed conversion and sales lift from Consumer FX — self-reported, and not yet verified by any independent party in what's public so far — holds up once it's running across Mollie's larger and more varied merchant base.
