## What Was Actually Agreed

On October 21, [Commerce.com](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/21/3385458/0/en/commerce-and-cloudflare-bring-ai-shopping-assistants-to-bigcommerce-merchants-online-stores-with-webmcp.html) — the e-commerce platform that renamed itself from BigCommerce in 2025 — and Cloudflare said Cloudflare has switched on WebMCP, an experimental browser standard, by default across nearly 100,000 of Commerce.com's merchant storefronts. Cloudflare injects a small script at its network edge so each store's real product catalog, prices, and inventory are exposed as callable "tools" — `search_products`, `add_to_cart` — that a WebMCP-aware browser agent can invoke directly, rather than screen-scraping the page and guessing. Merchants need no code changes; eligible stores get it automatically and can opt out in a settings menu. Checkout still redirects to the merchant's own site. [Cloudflare's own writeup](https://blog.cloudflare.com/webmcp-commerce/) frames this as solving a narrower problem too: WebMCP was built as a single on/off switch for an entire domain, and Cloudflare's script lets individual merchants control it instead. Neither company disclosed a fee tied to the integration.

## What Each Side Gets, and Gives Up

Cloudflare gets to be the default plumbing behind a fledgling standard, at the same moment it co-runs a different piece of agentic-commerce infrastructure: Visa's [Trusted Agent Protocol](https://investor.visa.com/news/news-details/2025/Visa-Introduces-Trusted-Agent-Protocol-An-Ecosystem-Led-Framework-for-AI-Commerce/), announced with Visa a year earlier, which verifies an agent's identity rather than exposing a store's tools. Sitting underneath both — one for identity, one for catalog access — is a hedge: Cloudflare doesn't have to bet on which agentic-commerce standard wins. Commerce.com gets to claim its merchants are "agent-ready" ahead of rivals like Shopify or Adobe Commerce, which fits a company that renamed itself specifically to signal an agentic-commerce pivot. What both give up is some control over who reads the data: once a store's catalog is exposed as a callable tool, any WebMCP-compliant agent can use it, not only ones either company has a relationship with. Financial terms of the Commerce.com–Cloudflare arrangement were not disclosed by either side.

## Why Now

The timing tracks the standard's own rollout more than merchant demand. Google [unveiled WebMCP](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/ai-webmcp-origin-trial) in February 2026 and opened a Chrome 149 origin trial in June; Cloudflare ran a smaller, manually configured beta in August; this week's move defaults it on at scale. That ordering matters because, as of this writing, no mainstream consumer AI agent — including Chrome's own Gemini assistant — reliably calls WebMCP tools in production; Google has said Gemini-in-Chrome support is coming "soon," without a firm date. Reading this as Commerce.com and Cloudflare positioning merchant infrastructure ahead of agent demand, rather than responding to it, is an interpretation, not something either company stated.

## Who It Affects

Other commerce platforms — Shopify, Adobe Commerce, Salesforce Commerce Cloud — face pressure to flip a comparable switch or cede the "agent-ready" claim to Commerce.com. It also lands inside a broader standards contest this site [covered earlier in October](/posts/career/meta-sierra-personal-agent-protocol): Meta and Sierra's Personal Agent Protocol governs how an agent gets permission to act; OpenAI and Stripe's Agentic Commerce Protocol governs in-agent payment; Visa and Cloudflare's Trusted Agent Protocol governs agent identity. WebMCP, now live on Commerce.com's base, is the layer underneath all of them: what an agent can actually ask a webpage to do. Shoppers who already use browser agents gain a cleaner path to real inventory; merchants gain exposure to an audience that doesn't yet meaningfully exist.

## What to Watch Next

Three things would turn this from a bet into a result: Gemini-in-Chrome or another mainstream agent actually calling WebMCP tools in production; a rival commerce platform matching Commerce.com's default-on move; and Commerce.com disclosing any measurable agent-driven traffic or sales from the ~100,000 enabled stores, rather than only the infrastructure switch itself.
